# Asking what is missing

## Once, all together

After drafting the file and running the check, ask **in one message** everything you could not tell from the material:
first what the check pointed out (`references/look.md`), then what the material left open, in this order — skipping
what you already know:

1. **Who leads** — the lanes (a role, a team, an area).
2. **How it starts** — where an item comes from, the first stage.
3. **The stages in order**, and who leads each.
4. **Where someone decides** — the gates, who approves each (and whether someone else also approves, in some
   case: "also", "if it is", "when it involves"), what must be ready.
5. **What each stage delivers.**
6. **Where a failed gate goes back.**
7. **The number** that says it worked (only when the flow delivers something measurable).

At most 6 questions in a round; the most important first. When more are open, the rest wait for the next round —
never two questions merged into one, never a question tucked outside the numbered list.

## Each question

- **Bet first, always**: your best answer, said so the person can just say yes — "I'd say the Tech Lead approves
  *Ready for production?* (page 4 says TL signs the release)". Say where the bet comes from (a document and its page,
  what they said, or that it is only a guess).
- Up to 3 options when the answer is a choice, your bet first. Never "Other" — the person can always answer in their
  own words.
- One thing per question. No jargon the material does not use.
- Number the questions so the person can answer "1 yes, 2 the PO, 3 skip".

## After the answer

Apply every answer, run the check again, and ask again only about what the answers opened — or close. What the person
skipped stays out of the file and goes in your closing summary as an open point. Never ask the same thing twice.

## When the person corrects you

A correction they state is applied, not asked ("Ana is in the platform team, not QA" → the stages move lane; Ana is not
a role). Then show what changed in one line.
